02 BRIEFING

Executive briefing

kaaroViewer began as an open-world knowledge graph explorer powered by NER and SPARQL, but failed at the boundary of private knowledge with no Wikidata analog. LLMs fill that gap by encoding arbitrary text into the intelligence brief format — but unconstrained LLM freedom causes ontology drift across encodings. The three-part lock (validator + SOP + renderer) and Dream Loop are the current architectural answer to a problem that is named, partially constrained, and still being refined.

Knowledge Meets the Open World

CONCEPT Open-World Assumption

The earliest kaaroViewer is built on an implicit assumption: knowledge worth visualizing exists in the open world. OpenTapioca matches text to Wikidata QIDs; SPARQL fetches structured facts — descriptions, images, linked identifiers. For public subjects, this works precisely because Wikidata already knows them. The system renders what it can look up.

A-Frame and the First Spatial Canvas

SOFTWARE A-Frame

The first rendering layer is A-Frame, a declarative WebXR framework. Public entities from Wikidata become primitives in a 3D scene. The canvas is functional but abstract — labels on geometric shapes. Spatial layout begins to suggest that geometry alone cannot carry meaning.

The Boundary Where Public Knowledge Ends

CONCEPT Private Knowledge Vault

Personal knowledge vaults, project notes, research documents — these texts have no QIDs. OpenTapioca cannot match them to Wikidata. SPARQL has nothing to retrieve. The open-world assumption fails at exactly the boundary where personal knowledge begins, and the system that works for public subjects cannot see private ones at all.

LLM as Ontology Constructor

STANDARD Intelligence Brief

An LLM is introduced to encode private text: identify entities, name types, trace relationships, construct narrative, surface insights. The intelligence brief format emerges — a seven-layer JSON document (meta, nodes, edges, story, insights, clusters, report_card) that becomes the system's lingua franca. The LLM can encode what NER cannot. The library grows beyond public knowledge into personal knowledge vaults.

Ontology Drift — The Liability of LLM Freedom

CONCEPT Ontology Drift

Left unconstrained, the LLM invents types and relationships the renderer does not understand. Two documents on the same topic, encoded weeks apart, produce incompatible schemas. Early encodings produce edge densities as low as 1.32; others reach 2.41. The gap is not quality variance — it is ontological incoherence. Drift becomes the dominant library failure mode.

Three.js — The Visual Contract Made Direct

SOFTWARE Three.js

A-Frame is superseded by a direct Three.js canvas providing full control: force-directed layout, tier-scaled geometry, sentiment aura rings, animated story beat transitions. Each node type maps to a geometry; each sentiment to an aura color. The visual contract makes the ontology concrete — a type that renders as a fallback primitive is visually anonymous. The renderer becomes an enforcement point, not just a display layer.

The Three-Part Lock — Ontology Made Defensible

CONCEPT Three-Part Lock

The three-part lock crystallizes: validator (canonical VALID_TYPES/VALID_RELS — 38 types, 29 rels), SOP reference (semantic + visual explanation), renderer (geometry/style switches) — all three must update atomically. The Dream Loop runs its first execution: the `reveals` relationship, repeatedly used from memory, is promoted from warning to blocking error. Across 4 Alone-Time runs, the library moves from 3 degraded entries to 9 ok · 0 watch · 0 degraded. The personalized ontology has its first defensible form.

Two Paths — Explore and Enforce

ALGORITHM Explore Pipeline

The explore pipeline offers anyone a draft knowledge graph in seconds — Gemini, a single LLM call, approximate ontology adherence. The visualize skill offers a power user a library-grade brief — three-pass encoding, full validator enforcement, permanent registration. The distinction is not quality alone: it is how much of the personalized ontology is enforced. Both paths produce intelligence briefs; only one enforces the full ontology contract.

Painting — Images for At-a-Glance Comprehension

SOFTWARE Paint System

The paint system adds a comprehension channel that geometry cannot provide. PaintContext assembles three inputs — spatial positioning (nodes in camera frustum), textual context (slide narration + node description), cinematic camera angle — into a prompt informed by stable diffusion seed prompt patterns. 4 named strategies (cinematic/documentary/abstract/blueprint) produce genuinely different images from the same canvas state. Gemini generates the image; Three.js applies it as a mesh texture.

15 INSIGHT
⚑ WARNING
HIGH
"LLM-encoded ontologies drift irreversibly without atomic updates to all three lock components — validator, SOP, and renderer"

The freedom that makes LLMs useful for encoding private knowledge is the same freedom that causes ontology drift. Early library entries show edge density variance from 1.32 to 2.41 — not quality variance but ontological incoherence. The three-part lock prevents silent divergence: when the validator's vocabulary, the SOP's semantic rules, and the renderer's visual contract all agree, encoders produce consistent graphs. When they diverge, a single missing renderer branch creates visually anonymous nodes that no encoder can detect.

16 INSIGHT
◈ FINDING
MEDIUM
"The explore pipeline and visualize skill enforce different fractions of the ontology by design — conflating them produces neither speed nor quality"

The explore pipeline approximates the ontology for speed and accessibility; the visualize skill enforces it fully for library permanence. These are not the same tool at different quality levels — they serve different users at different stages of the knowledge lifecycle. Treating explore output as library-grade is the most common misuse. Treating every encoding as requiring visualize-skill depth is the most common bottleneck.

17 INSIGHT
◎ PATTERN
MEDIUM
"Spatial positioning, textual slide context, and cinematic camera angle together are sufficient to generate domain-appropriate images — stable diffusion seed prompt patterns transfer directly to knowledge graph painting"

The paint system's three-input prompt construction (spatial + textual + cinematic) reproduces key dimensions that the stable diffusion community discovered empirically. The named strategies — cinematic, documentary, abstract, blueprint — are domain-specific instantiations of well-known compositional patterns, not ad hoc engineering. This transferability means the paint system can be improved by importing prompt engineering research rather than rediscovering it from scratch.
